Painting

From walkthrough to signed job to paid crew.

Painting companies win on speed to estimate and lose margin in the gap between the bid and the hours. We work on both ends.

Where the hours goWhat we automate
Estimates written at night after the walkthroughEstimates sent the same day, with follow-ups queued
Follow-ups that depend on someone rememberingFollow-ups on schedule until the job is signed or closed
Job costing done weeks later in a spreadsheetDollar per hour for every crew, every week
Drywall

Multi-visit jobs that stay in order.

Drywall is a sequence: hang, tape, mud and sand, often over several visits with drying time between. When one stage slips, every visit after it slips too.

Where the hours goWhat we automate
Visits rescheduled by phone when a stage runs longLater visits moved automatically when a stage slips
Materials tracked loosely against the jobMaterial use recorded per job and compared to the estimate
Change orders lost between the site and the officeChange orders logged to the job and carried to the invoice
Pest control

Routes, recurring service and quality on every stop.

Pest control runs on recurring routes. The hours go into building them, rebuilding them when the day changes, and checking the work afterwards.

Where the hours goWhat we automate
Routes built one technician at a timeRoutes balanced across every technician at once
Quality control on a sample of work ordersEvery work order read for notes and material usage
Commercial accounts followed up from memoryFollow-ups scheduled for every commercial account
Home services

Dispatch, calls and payroll without the desk work.

Home service teams spend the office day on the phone and the end of the week on payroll. Both are mostly moving information from one system to another.

Where the hours goWhat we automate

Payroll rebuilt from several exports every weekHours and bonuses combined into one sheet for payroll
